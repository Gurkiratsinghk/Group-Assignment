# EDA Findings: Distribution of range_pct

`range_pct` is not normally distributed, and the departure is not subtle. The population (n = 5,928) has skewness of 2.46 and excess kurtosis of 11.85 — a strong right skew with a heavy right tail, consistent with a variable bounded at zero (a trading range cannot be negative) that occasionally spikes on volatile sessions. The histogram and Q-Q plot confirm this: the Q-Q plot bows sharply upward at the right tail, and Anderson-Darling (A² = 210.0) rejects normality at every tabulated significance level, as do the D'Agostino K² test (p ≈ 0) and Shapiro-Wilk on a 500-row random subsample (W = 0.783, p ≈ 3×10⁻²⁵). Shapiro-Wilk cannot be run on the full population because scipy's implementation becomes unreliable and impractically powerful above roughly 5,000 observations — at n = 5,928 it would flag any minuscule, practically meaningless deviation as significant, so a seeded subsample is the more honest test.

This does not disqualify a t-based approach at n = 40. The variable being non-normal and the sampling distribution of its mean being approximately normal are different claims. The Central Limit Theorem says that as sample size grows, the distribution of the sample mean approaches normality regardless of the parent distribution's shape — it says nothing about the raw data itself. With n = 40 and skew of this magnitude, CLT convergence is imperfect but generally adequate for the mean's sampling distribution, so t-procedures remain defensible for inference on means, with appropriate caution.

Levene's test (center = median) rejects equal variances between Technology (variance ≈ 1.286) and Consumer Staples (variance ≈ 1.009), W = 15.58, p < 0.001. This rules out the pooled-variance t-test and points to Welch's t-test, which does not assume equal variances.

If sample sizes were smaller or skew this severe made even the mean's sampling distribution suspect, Wilcoxon signed-rank (one-sample/paired) or Mann-Whitney U (two-sample) would be the safer fallback, since they test medians/ranks rather than relying on normality of means.
