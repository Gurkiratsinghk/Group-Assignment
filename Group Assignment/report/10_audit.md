# Step 10: Sceptical Audit of the Full Project

This audit assumes errors are present and goes looking for them, rather than confirming what the author already believed. It covers reproducibility, independent recomputation by a different method, a bootstrap cross-check, a targeted error hunt, and a review of which numbers were ever independently verified. Full detail and console output: `scripts/10_audit.py`.

## 1. Reproducibility

Re-running `scripts/01_acquire_data.py` against the live Yahoo Finance API does **not** reproduce the committed data exactly: `adj_close` drifts by up to 0.00146 on 1,981 of 5,928 rows between calls (Yahoo revises its adjustment factors over time). However, `open`/`high`/`low`/`close` — and therefore every variable actually used in this project (`range_pct`, `daily_return_pct`, the gap/move variables) — were byte-identical across both pulls, and the one `adj_close`-dependent check (the ex-dividend hazard count) returned the identical count (22) both times. This is a real, documented non-determinism in the data source, but it does not propagate to any reported statistic. All downstream scripts (02, 03, 04, 06, 07, 08) were re-run twice against the fixed `population.csv` and every one of 23 output tables/sample files regenerated byte-identical.

## 2. Independent recomputation (different method)

Step 1's mean/s/SE, Step 2's CI bounds, Step 5's t/p, and all three Step 6 t-tests were recomputed using `statsmodels` (`DescrStatsW`, `CompareMeans`, OLS regression) instead of the original `scipy`/manual-arithmetic path. Every quantity agreed to machine precision (max absolute difference 3.0×10⁻¹⁵; see `outputs/tables/10_independent_recomputation.csv`).

## 3. Bootstrap cross-check

A 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap on the Step 2 sample gives a 95% CI of [1.677%, 2.390%], versus the t-based [1.633%, 2.375%]. The two broadly agree, but the bootstrap interval is visibly shifted right and asymmetric (wider on top) — it inherits the sample's own right skew (skewness 1.95), while the t-interval is forced to be symmetric around the mean by construction. Given the confirmed skew, the bootstrap interval is arguably the more honest of the two.

## 5. Numbers accepted without independent check

Shapiro-Wilk, D'Agostino K², Anderson-Darling, Wilcoxon, and Mann-Whitney statistics have no convenient independent closed form and remain single-library-call values, flagged here rather than silently accepted. Two gaps *were* closed in this audit: Step 6's Cohen's d values (Test B and C) were verified against the algebraic identity linking t to d (both matched to float precision), and Step 4's observed-effect-size power (0.0523) was verified by direct 10,000-trial Monte Carlo simulation at that exact effect size (Monte Carlo: 0.0518 — agrees closely).

## Corrected values: the identical-sample bug, before and after

| Quantity | Before fix | After fix |
|---|---|---|
| Levene p-value | 0.028 | 0.665 |
| Adopted test | Welch | Pooled |
| t statistic | 1.964 | -0.782 |
| p-value | 0.054 | 0.437 |
| Cohen's d | 0.44 | -0.17 |
| Mean difference (IT − FMCG) | +0.427 pp | -0.236 pp |

The decision happens to be unchanged (fail to reject both times), but the effect reverses direction and collapses from a borderline-moderate size to a small, clearly null one. This is the concrete demonstration of why the bug mattered: it was not a harmless duplication, it was reporting the wrong sign and an inflated effect size for a result the desk could plausibly have acted on. `report/08_step6.md` and `report/09_synthesis.md` have been corrected accordingly.
